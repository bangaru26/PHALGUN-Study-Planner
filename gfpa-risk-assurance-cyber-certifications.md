# GFPA Professional Risk, Assurance & Information Security Pathways
**PHALGUN / GFPA curriculum specification**  
**Status:** Curriculum pathway added; individual lessons, practice banks, eligibility checks and exam readiness must be authored and validated before this is represented as a completed course or certification.

This is an original learning pathway designed to build knowledge and practical skills aligned to the publicly available topic outlines of the Institute of Risk Management (IRM), The Institute of Internal Auditors (IIA), and ISACA. It is **not** an official provider course, an endorsement, a guarantee of passing, or a substitute for checking current exam syllabi, experience requirements, membership rules, fees, and certification policies.

## Learning architecture
GFPA keeps its existing finance and accounting curriculum. These pathways extend it with enterprise risk management, assurance, internal audit, and information-security governance. Learners progress through:
1. Foundations and vocabulary
2. Guided concepts and worked examples
3. Practical tools and synthetic case exercises
4. Scenario-based quizzes with answer rationales
5. Integrated capstone and portfolio evidence
6. Syllabus mapping and readiness review against current official material

## A. Institute of Risk Management (IRM) — progressive pathway across all qualification stages
Map learning to the current IRM qualification structure and official syllabi before each cohort or exam cycle. The stages below are GFPA's teaching sequence, not a claim that IRM formally names its awards by these exact stage labels.

### Stage 1 — Risk foundations
- Risk, uncertainty, objectives, opportunity and threat; strategic, operational, financial, legal/compliance, reputational, people, technology, cyber, third-party, project, climate and emerging risks.
- Risk appetite, tolerance, capacity, velocity, likelihood, impact, inherent risk, controls, residual risk and target risk.
- Risk ownership, governance, accountability, the board, executives, risk function and the three-lines model.
- Risk culture, conduct, ethics, stakeholder expectations and risk-aware decision-making.
- Risk registers, simple heat maps, assumptions, limitations and escalation thresholds.

### Stage 2 — Applied risk management / certificate-level knowledge
- Establish context, identify risks, analyse and evaluate them, select responses, implement controls, monitor and communicate.
- Qualitative and quantitative assessment; scoring limitations; scenario analysis, sensitivity analysis, bow-tie analysis, root-cause analysis and key risk indicators.
- Risk response: avoid, reduce/mitigate, share/transfer and accept; cost-benefit and residual-risk approval.
- Business continuity, crisis management, incident response, recovery and lessons learned.
- Practical risk register, control library, risk owner interview and management report.

### Stage 3 — Enterprise risk management / international certificate-level knowledge
- Integrating risk with strategy, planning, performance, budgeting and major decisions.
- Risk appetite statements, risk aggregation, interdependencies, concentration risk and portfolio views.
- Governance, assurance mapping, risk reporting, culture assessment and risk maturity.
- Supply-chain, outsourcing, project/change, fraud, data, cyber, AI, ESG/climate and geopolitical risk scenarios.
- Build a risk framework for a fictional Indian business and a multinational shared-services operation.

### Stage 4 — Advanced / diploma-level application
- Design and critique an enterprise risk management framework; connect strategy, risk, controls, resilience and assurance.
- Evaluate complex and interconnected risks, strategic options, uncertainty, risk-adjusted decisions and emerging threats.
- Risk financing, insurance and transfer limitations; third-party dependencies; resilience and continuity planning.
- Board and committee reporting, risk appetite breaches, challenge, escalation and assurance over risk management.
- Case-study analysis, executive recommendations and a defensible risk treatment plan.

### Stage 5 — Professional integration and continuing development
- Integrated risk, audit, compliance, internal control, cybersecurity and business resilience.
- Facilitate risk workshops; assess risk culture; challenge assumptions without overstating evidence.
- Design KRIs, control indicators, dashboards and management reporting using Excel, SQL, Power BI and Python.
- Complete a capstone: strategy map → risk taxonomy → assessment → treatment → controls → KRI dashboard → assurance plan → board paper.
- Maintain a dated syllabus crosswalk to the official IRM qualification pages; confirm current qualification names, formal levels, assessment methods and eligibility before advertising exam alignment.

## B. Certified Internal Auditor (CIA) — The IIA
Use the current official CIA syllabus and candidate handbook as the source of truth. Organise learning into the current three-part structure, refreshing this mapping whenever The IIA changes it.

### CIA Part 1 — Internal Audit Fundamentals
- Foundations, purpose, mandate, independence, objectivity, ethics and professional scepticism.
- Governance, risk management, internal control and applicable Global Internal Audit Standards.
- Fraud awareness, compliance, assurance vs consulting, and the internal auditor's responsibilities and limits.
- Scenario questions that require selecting the most appropriate professional action.

### CIA Part 2 — Internal Audit Engagement
- Engagement planning, objectives, scope, criteria, risk assessment and work programmes.
- Process walkthroughs, risk-and-control matrices, design assessment, sampling and evidence reliability.
- Test of design and operating effectiveness, workpapers, exceptions, root cause and conclusions.
- Findings, recommendations, management action plans, communication and follow-up.

### CIA Part 3 — Internal Audit Function
- Internal audit function management, strategy, resources, quality and performance.
- Governance, risk management, control, business acumen, organisational behaviour and technology concepts as specified by the current syllabus.
- Data analytics, IT/cybersecurity concepts, business processes and financial/management topics.
- Integrated practice exams, timed review, error log and domain-by-domain readiness tracking.

## C. Certification in Risk Management Assurance (CRMA) — The IIA
- Governance and risk oversight; board and audit committee responsibilities.
- Risk culture, appetite, tolerance, strategic alignment and management accountability.
- Risk management principles, frameworks, identification, analysis, response, monitoring and reporting.
- Evaluating the design and effectiveness of enterprise risk management processes.
- Risk-based internal audit planning and assurance coordination across internal audit, compliance, risk, external audit and other providers.
- Emerging risk, change, project risk, third-party risk, data privacy, cybersecurity and analytics.
- Trusted-advisor communication, challenge, assurance conclusions and recommendations.
- Practice scenarios must follow the current official CRMA syllabus; note the version/standards basis and any transition guidance published by The IIA.

## D. Certified Information Security Manager (CISM) — ISACA
CISM is management-focused: governance, risk, security programme management and incident management. It is not a hands-on penetration-testing course.

### Domain 1 — Information Security Governance
- Enterprise governance, organisational culture, legal/regulatory/contractual requirements, roles and accountability.
- Information-security strategy, frameworks, policy hierarchy, business alignment, budget and business cases.
- Security metrics, stakeholder communication and executive reporting.

### Domain 2 — Information Security Risk Management
- Assets, threats, vulnerabilities, control weaknesses and risk assessment.
- Risk analysis, prioritisation, treatment, acceptance and ownership.
- Monitoring, reporting, emerging risks, third-party risk and integration with enterprise risk management.

### Domain 3 — Information Security Programme
- Programme strategy, architecture awareness, resources, asset classification and security frameworks.
- Policy, standards, control selection, implementation, integration, testing and evaluation.
- Awareness, metrics, suppliers, programme communications and continual improvement.
- Identity/access, data protection, cloud, secure change, logging and control assurance at management level.

### Domain 4 — Incident Management
- Incident readiness, classification, escalation, response roles and communications.
- Business impact analysis, business continuity and disaster recovery alignment.
- Detection, investigation, containment, eradication, recovery and post-incident review.
- Tabletop exercises, incident metrics, lessons learned and risk reassessment.

**Time-sensitive syllabus control:** ISACA announced that the CISM exam content outline changes effective **3 November 2026**. The learning pathway must clearly distinguish the pre-change and updated outlines, and learners preparing for exams on/after that date should use the updated official materials. Confirm the live outline before each study cycle.

## E. Cross-pathway practical labs
1. **Finance operations risk:** Map procure-to-pay, order-to-cash and record-to-report risks; build an RCM and test synthetic transactions.
2. **Enterprise risk register:** Create risk statements, causes, consequences, inherent/residual scores, owners, treatments and KRIs.
3. **Risk assurance map:** Match strategic risks to management controls, risk-function monitoring, compliance and internal audit assurance.
4. **Cybersecurity governance tabletop:** Simulate a ransomware/third-party outage; produce an escalation timeline, executive brief and recovery actions without conducting unauthorised technical testing.
5. **Board risk paper:** Summarise top risks, appetite breaches, trend indicators, treatment decisions and assurance gaps in a concise board-ready format.
6. **Integrated capstone:** A fictional organisation faces a material supplier failure, suspicious payment patterns and a data-security incident. Learners analyse risks, define control ownership, plan audit assurance, manage incident governance and present recommendations.

## F. Assessment standard
Every lesson should contain:
- Learning objectives and prerequisites
- Plain-language explanation and glossary
- Worked example and realistic but synthetic case data
- Guided practice plus independent exercise
- Scenario quiz with answer explanations, including why distractors are wrong
- Applied artefact (risk register, RCM, audit workpaper, board paper, incident playbook or dashboard)
- Source list with publisher, URL, version/date checked and next review date
- Cumulative assessment and a record of weak domains for spaced revision
