# Fintech Kalp — Chapter 1 Learning Pack
## Accounting Foundations: the accounting equation, double entry, accruals and month-end close

**Purpose:** Build the accounting foundation needed for finance operations, reconciliations, reporting and later analytics modules.

**Learning outcomes**
By the end of this chapter, the learner can:
1. Classify common transactions as assets, liabilities, equity, income or expenses.
2. Explain and apply Assets = Liabilities + Equity.
3. Prepare balanced double-entry journal entries.
4. Distinguish cash accounting from accrual accounting.
5. Explain prepayments, accruals and depreciation.
6. Prepare a basic trial balance and identify why a balanced trial balance may still contain errors.
7. Describe a controlled month-end close.

**Suggested study time:** 3–4 hours across two or three sessions.

---

## A. Plain-language notes

### 1. The five account groups
- **Assets:** resources controlled by the business, such as cash, bank, inventory, receivables and equipment.
- **Liabilities:** amounts the business owes, such as supplier payables, loans and accrued expenses.
- **Equity:** the owners' residual interest, including share capital and retained earnings.
- **Income:** amounts earned from ordinary activities and other gains.
- **Expenses:** costs incurred to earn income or operate the business.

A customer who has been invoiced but has not paid creates a **receivable (asset)**. A supplier invoice received but not yet paid creates a **payable (liability)**.

### 2. The accounting equation
**Assets = Liabilities + Equity**

Example: A business has cash of ₹80,000, equipment of ₹20,000 and a bank loan of ₹30,000. Assets are ₹100,000. Equity must be ₹70,000 because ₹100,000 = ₹30,000 + ₹70,000.

Every correctly recorded transaction preserves the equation.

### 3. Debit and credit
Debit means the left side of an account; credit means the right side. They do not automatically mean increase and decrease.

| Account type | Increase | Decrease |
|---|---|---|
| Asset | Debit | Credit |
| Expense | Debit | Credit |
| Liability | Credit | Debit |
| Equity | Credit | Debit |
| Income | Credit | Debit |

Every journal entry has equal total debits and credits. This is the double-entry principle.

### 4. Worked journal entries
**Owner invests ₹500,000 into the business**
- Dr Bank ₹500,000
- Cr Share Capital ₹500,000

**Equipment bought for cash, ₹80,000**
- Dr Equipment ₹80,000
- Cr Bank ₹80,000

**Services provided on credit, ₹25,000**
- Dr Trade Receivables ₹25,000
- Cr Service Revenue ₹25,000

**Customer pays ₹10,000**
- Dr Bank ₹10,000
- Cr Trade Receivables ₹10,000

The receivable still outstanding is ₹15,000. Receiving cash settles an asset; it does not create revenue a second time.

### 5. Accrual accounting
Record income when it is earned and expenses when they are incurred, subject to the applicable accounting requirements—not simply when cash is received or paid.

- **Accrued expense:** expense incurred but not yet invoiced or paid. Example: electricity used in March, invoice received in April.
- **Prepayment:** cash paid before the related service is consumed. Example: annual insurance paid in advance.
- **Deferred/unearned income:** cash received before the business has fulfilled the related performance obligation.
- **Depreciation:** systematic allocation of a depreciable asset's amount over its useful life; it is not a valuation of the asset's current market price.

**Depreciation example:** Cost ₹120,000; residual value ₹12,000; useful life 36 months. Straight-line monthly depreciation = (₹120,000 − ₹12,000) ÷ 36 = ₹3,000.

### 6. Trial balance and errors
A trial balance lists ledger balances and checks whether total debits equal total credits. It can still balance despite:
- an entire transaction being omitted;
- a transaction being posted to the wrong account;
- equal amounts being posted to the wrong accounts;
- a transaction being recorded twice on both sides;
- a compensating pair of errors.

A balanced trial balance is a useful check, not proof that the accounts are correct.

### 7. Month-end close
A typical close includes:
1. Confirm period cut-off and transaction completeness.
2. Close or reconcile subledgers such as AP, AR and fixed assets.
3. Reconcile bank accounts and key balance-sheet accounts.
4. Record supported accruals, prepayments and depreciation.
5. Review intercompany balances and unusual movements.
6. Prepare journals with supporting evidence and independent approval.
7. Review trial balance, analytical variances and financial statements.
8. Obtain close sign-off and lock the period under the organisation's policy.

Do not post a balancing “plug” merely to make a reconciliation agree. Investigate, document, approve and retain evidence.

## B. Worked example — mini trial balance

| Account | Debit (₹) | Credit (₹) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bank | 50,000 | — |
| Trade receivables | 30,000 | — |
| Equipment | 40,000 | — |
| Trade payables | — | 20,000 |
| Share capital | — | 70,000 |
| Revenue | — | 45,000 |
| Expenses | 25,000 | — |
| **Total** | **145,000** | **135,000** |

**Important teaching check:** The figures above do not balance. The learner must not force a plug. Investigate the source balances. If the stated credit balance for share capital were ₹80,000 instead of ₹70,000, both totals would be ₹145,000. This illustrates why a trial balance is a check, not an exercise in changing figures to fit.

## C. Practice assignment

Use the following independent transactions for a small services business. Ignore GST and tax for this exercise.

1. Owner deposits ₹300,000 into the business bank account.
2. Business buys a laptop for ₹60,000, paid from bank.
3. Business purchases stationery for ₹4,000 on supplier credit.
4. Business provides services worth ₹45,000 on credit.
5. Customer pays ₹20,000 against the invoice in transaction 4.
6. Business pays the stationery supplier ₹2,500.
7. Month-end electricity of ₹3,000 has been consumed but not invoiced.
8. Monthly depreciation on the laptop is ₹1,500.

**Tasks**
- Prepare the journal entry for each transaction.
- Identify the account group for every account used.
- Calculate the closing receivable and stationery payable.
- Calculate the total revenue and expenses recognised from these transactions.
- Explain why the customer receipt in transaction 5 is not additional revenue.
- Explain why the electricity expense is recognised even though no invoice has arrived.
- List four checks or pieces of evidence you would retain before sign-off.

**Model answer**
1. Dr Bank 300,000; Cr Owner's Capital 300,000.
2. Dr Computer Equipment 60,000; Cr Bank 60,000.
3. Dr Stationery Expense 4,000; Cr Trade Payables 4,000.
4. Dr Trade Receivables 45,000; Cr Service Revenue 45,000.
5. Dr Bank 20,000; Cr Trade Receivables 20,000.
6. Dr Trade Payables 2,500; Cr Bank 2,500.
7. Dr Electricity Expense 3,000; Cr Accrued Expenses 3,000.
8. Dr Depreciation Expense 1,500; Cr Accumulated Depreciation 1,500.

Closing receivable = ₹25,000. Closing stationery payable = ₹1,500. Revenue = ₹45,000. Expenses recognised = ₹8,500 (stationery ₹4,000 + electricity ₹3,000 + depreciation ₹1,500). The customer receipt reduces the receivable; it does not earn the service revenue again. Electricity was consumed in the period, so accrual accounting recognises the expense and liability. Evidence examples: customer invoice/service completion evidence; bank statement; supplier invoice/payment evidence; electricity usage estimate and later invoice; laptop invoice and fixed-asset record; approved journal support.

## D. Multiple-choice knowledge check

Choose one answer for each question. The answer key includes a short explanation.

1. Which equation is fundamental to double-entry accounting?
   - A. Revenue = Expenses + Cash
   - B. Assets = Liabilities + Equity
   - C. Assets = Revenue − Expenses
   - D. Cash = Equity − Liabilities

2. A customer invoice issued for services already provided normally records:
   - A. Dr Revenue, Cr Bank
   - B. Dr Receivables, Cr Revenue
   - C. Dr Bank, Cr Receivables
   - D. Dr Payables, Cr Revenue

3. Which account normally increases with a debit?
   - A. Revenue
   - B. Trade payables
   - C. Equipment
   - D. Share capital

4. A supplier invoice received but unpaid is generally:
   - A. A receivable
   - B. A liability
   - C. Equity
   - D. Cash income

5. A customer pays ₹10,000 against an existing receivable. What is the correct effect?
   - A. Increase revenue by ₹10,000
   - B. Increase bank and reduce receivables
   - C. Increase payables and reduce bank
   - D. Increase equity directly

6. Electricity consumed this month but invoiced next month is generally:
   - A. Ignored until paid
   - B. Recorded as an accrued expense and liability
   - C. Recorded as equipment
   - D. Recorded as revenue

7. Annual insurance paid in advance is initially usually:
   - A. A prepayment asset
   - B. A trade receivable
   - C. Share capital
   - D. Revenue

8. Straight-line monthly depreciation for cost ₹60,000, residual value ₹6,000 and useful life 36 months is:
   - A. ₹1,000
   - B. ₹1,500
   - C. ₹1,667
   - D. ₹2,000

9. Which statement about a trial balance is correct?
   - A. If it balances, no errors exist
   - B. It proves every transaction is genuine
   - C. It checks equality of debit and credit balances but cannot detect every error
   - D. It replaces reconciliations

10. What should an accountant do when a reconciliation difference is unexplained?
    - A. Post a plug
    - B. Delete the unmatched item
    - C. Investigate, document and escalate/approve the correction
    - D. Change the bank balance

11. Which is normally an asset?
    - A. Trade receivable
    - B. Trade payable
    - C. Electricity expense
    - D. Service revenue

12. Which close activity is most directly related to period cut-off?
    - A. Confirming transactions belong in the correct accounting period
    - B. Changing the chart of accounts without approval
    - C. Deleting old support
    - D. Skipping unbilled expenses

13. A journal entry must normally satisfy:
    - A. Credits exceed debits
    - B. Debits equal credits
    - C. Every entry affects cash
    - D. Every entry affects revenue

14. Depreciation is best described as:
    - A. A cash payment every month
    - B. Allocation of depreciable amount over useful life
    - C. A guaranteed market valuation
    - D. A supplier payment

15. Which evidence is most useful to support a bank reconciliation?
    - A. An unsupported estimate
    - B. Bank statement, cashbook/GL and documented reconciling items
    - C. A verbal assurance only
    - D. Last year's trial balance only

**Answer key**
1. B — the accounting equation links assets, liabilities and equity.
2. B — the customer owes the business, and revenue has been earned.
3. C — assets increase with debits.
4. B — the business owes the supplier.
5. B — cash/bank rises and receivables fall; revenue was recognised earlier.
6. B — recognise the period's expense and related liability.
7. A — the unused coverage is a future economic benefit.
8. B — (₹60,000 − ₹6,000) ÷ 36 = ₹1,500.
9. C — omissions and classification errors may remain undetected.
10. C — corrections require evidence and proper approval.
11. A — a receivable is a right to collect cash.
12. A — cut-off assigns transactions to the proper period.
13. B — total debits and credits must agree.
14. B — depreciation allocates cost less residual value over useful life.
15. B — reconcile independent source evidence to the ledger.

**Suggested pass standard:** 12/15 (80%). Revisit the explanations for any incorrect answer before proceeding.

## E. Flashcards

| Front | Back |
|---|---|
| What is the accounting equation? | Assets = Liabilities + Equity. |
| What is an asset? | A resource controlled by the business from which future economic benefits are expected, subject to the applicable framework. |
| What is a liability? | A present obligation of the business to transfer an economic resource. |
| What is equity? | The residual interest in assets after deducting liabilities. |
| What is double-entry bookkeeping? | Recording each transaction with equal total debits and credits. |
| Which balances normally increase with a debit? | Assets and expenses. |
| Which balances normally increase with a credit? | Liabilities, equity and income. |
| What is a trade receivable? | Amount due from a customer. |
| What is a trade payable? | Amount owed to a supplier. |
| What is accrual accounting? | Recognising transactions in the periods in which the related income is earned or expenses incurred, rather than only when cash moves. |
| What is an accrued expense? | An expense incurred but not yet invoiced or paid. |
| What is a prepayment? | Cash paid before the related goods or services are consumed. |
| What is unearned income? | Cash received before the related goods or services have been provided. |
| What is depreciation? | Systematic allocation of a depreciable asset's amount over its useful life. |
| What is residual value? | Estimated amount recoverable at the end of useful life, net of disposal costs where relevant. |
| What is a trial balance? | A listing of ledger debit and credit balances used to check their arithmetic equality. |
| Does a balanced trial balance prove there are no errors? | No. Some omissions, wrong-account postings and compensating errors can remain. |
| What is cut-off? | Ensuring transactions are recorded in the correct accounting period. |
| What is a reconciliation? | Comparing independent records or balances, explaining differences and retaining evidence. |
| What is a journal approval control? | Independent review/authorisation of a journal before posting, according to policy. |

## F. Practical workplace and interview prompts

1. Explain the difference between a receivable and a payable using a business example.
2. Why does receiving cash from a customer not always mean revenue is earned on that date?
3. A trial balance is out by ₹12,000. Describe your investigation without posting a plug.
4. Explain an accrued expense to a non-finance colleague.
5. Describe the evidence you would retain for a month-end depreciation journal.

**Portfolio evidence to save:** completed journal workbook; trial balance with the deliberate imbalance identified; corrected version only after source evidence is supplied; MCQ score; one-page close checklist.

**Quality rule:** Never alter source balances just to force agreement. Record the issue, source, owner, evidence, action, approval and resolution.
