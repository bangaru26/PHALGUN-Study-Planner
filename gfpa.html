Try a starter lesson: Treasury & cash visibility

A short sample demonstrates the teaching style. Later lessons should add richer datasets, downloadable workpapers, answer rationales and mastery tracking.

Worked example — cash position versus profit

A company begins the day with ₹8,00,000 in bank. Expected customer receipts are ₹2,40,000; approved supplier payments are ₹3,10,000; payroll of ₹1,20,000 is due today. Assume all listed cash flows settle today and there are no other movements.

Formula: Closing cash = opening available cash + receipts − payments.

Calculation: ₹8,00,000 + ₹2,40,000 − ₹3,10,000 − ₹1,20,000 = ₹6,10,000.

Control thought: distinguish book balance from available funds; verify value dates, uncleared items, restricted cash, facility availability and payment cut-offs before using a forecast operationally.

Dedicated Module — US vs UK vs Indian Accounting

Global Accounting Standards & Conversion · Foundation to advanced · Designed for finance roles serving US, UK and Indian companies. This module is a learning comparison, not legal, tax or reporting advice. Always confirm the entity's applicable framework, jurisdiction, reporting period and current authoritative standards.

1 · Know the framework before posting

  • United States: US GAAP, primarily FASB Accounting Standards Codification for nongovernmental entities; SEC requirements apply to relevant public issuers. US tax accounting is separate from financial reporting.
  • United Kingdom: depending on entity and reporting purpose, UK-adopted international accounting standards or UK GAAP (for example, FRS 102/FRS 105 where eligible). Do not assume every UK company uses IFRS.
  • India: Ind AS applies to specified classes of companies; other entities use the applicable Indian Accounting Standards framework. Companies Act, MCA rules and relevant regulator requirements also matter.

Golden rule: identify the legal entity, reporting basis, group instructions, period, material accounting policies and reporting currency first. Do not select a treatment merely because it is familiar from another country.

3 · Worked conversion case: inventory

A business buys 100 units at ₹100 and then 100 units at ₹120. It sells 100 units. Under a periodic system, FIFO gives cost of goods sold of ₹10,000 and closing inventory of ₹12,000. LIFO would give cost of goods sold of ₹12,000 and closing inventory of ₹10,000. US GAAP may permit LIFO when its requirements are met; IFRS and Ind AS prohibit LIFO. A real group conversion must validate the actual costing system, applicable local framework and policy—not simply post a plug.

Month-End Close Lab — reconciliations, accruals and review

Authored practical lesson · intermediate level · fictional company. A close is not just posting journals: it is a controlled sequence of reconciliations, estimates, review and sign-off with traceable evidence. Apply the actual accounting framework and approved close policy in a real engagement.

1 · Bank reconciliation exception

At month-end, the general-ledger cash balance is ₹4,82,500. The bank statement shows ₹4,96,000. A ₹1,500 bank fee appears on the statement but is missing from the books. A valid ₹15,000 deposit in transit is recorded in the books but has not yet reached the statement. Assume no other reconciling items.

Book side: ₹4,82,500 − ₹1,500 bank fee = ₹4,81,000 adjusted book balance.

Bank side: ₹4,96,000 + ₹15,000 deposit in transit = ₹5,11,000 adjusted bank balance.

These balances do not agree. That is an unresolved exception, not permission to force a plug journal. Recheck the stated facts, cut-off, omitted transactions, signs, and any missing reconciling items before sign-off.

2 · Accrual journal

A service worth ₹24,000 was received before month-end. The supplier invoice has not arrived, and the cost is reliably measurable. Assume the applicable policy requires accrual accounting and no other adjustment is needed.

3 · Close control checklist

  • Reconcile material balance-sheet accounts to independent evidence and investigate aged reconciling items.
  • Review cut-off, accruals, prepayments, estimates, depreciation and inventory adjustments using approved policy.
  • Reconcile subledgers to the general ledger; explain material movements and unexpected balances.
  • Document preparer, reviewer, evidence link, date, exceptions, owner and resolution.
  • Restrict journal posting and approval rights; retain an auditable trail for manual and late journals.
  • Confirm unresolved items are escalated and sign-off criteria are met before closing the period.

4 · Close readiness decision

The bank reconciliation remains out of balance and the cause is not known. What is the defensible status?

Close discipline: reconciliation is not merely arithmetic. Preserve source evidence, avoid unsupported journals, evaluate materiality under policy, and escalate exceptions. This scenario intentionally contains an unexplained difference; the correct response is investigation rather than inventing a balancing item.

Internal Audit Lab — risk, control design and evidence

Authored practical lesson · intermediate level · fictional procurement process. Learn to move from a business objective to a risk statement, a control, a test procedure and a defensible conclusion. This is educational practice; adapt audit work to the approved charter, methodology, applicable standards and local requirements.

1 · Define the risk precisely

Objective: supplier invoices are valid, accurately recorded and paid only once with appropriate approval.

Risk statement: duplicate or unauthorised supplier invoices may be paid because invoice matching, approval and duplicate detection controls are incomplete or bypassed, causing financial loss and misstated liabilities.

2 · Evaluate the control

Control described by process owner: the ERP blocks exact duplicate invoice numbers for the same supplier; AP reviews duplicate warnings; invoices above the approval threshold require an authorised approver; exceptions are logged and resolved before payment.

A control description alone is not evidence that the control operated. The auditor should establish design, implementation and operating effectiveness, using reliable evidence and a documented, risk-based approach.

3 · Record an evidence-led finding

Illustrative test result: in a sample of 40 payments, two duplicate-warning exceptions lacked documented resolution before payment release. Do not extrapolate this sample result automatically to the entire population. Validate the facts, understand the cause and exposure, assess compensating controls, and discuss the evidence with the owner.

4 · Risk and reporting workpaper

  • Condition: two sampled exceptions lacked documented resolution before payment release.
  • Criteria: approved AP procedure and control design, confirmed against the applicable policy.
  • Cause: to be established through evidence, not assumed.
  • Effect/exposure: validate whether duplicate or unauthorised payments occurred and quantify any loss or liability impact.
  • Action: assign an owner and due date for exception workflow remediation; independently retest closure.

Professional judgement: use sufficient, relevant and reliable evidence; document scope, sampling rationale, limitations and review; protect confidential data; escalate suspected fraud through authorised channels. An audit finding should be fair, traceable and proportionate to verified facts.

Working Capital Lab — cash conversion cycle

Authored practical lesson · intermediate level · fictional distributor. Use operating data to connect receivables, inventory and supplier terms to cash pressure. A shorter cycle can release cash, but changes must not damage service, supply continuity or supplier relationships.

1 · Calculate the current cycle

Northstar Distribution reports average collection time (DSO) of 42 days, average inventory holding time (DIO) of 55 days, and average supplier payment time (DPO) of 35 days.

Formula: Cash conversion cycle (CCC) = DSO + DIO − DPO.

Worked calculation: 42 + 55 − 35 = 62 days. In this simplified illustration, cash is tied up in the operating cycle for about 62 days between paying for inventory and collecting from customers.

2 · Test an improvement scenario

A cross-functional plan targets DSO 37 days, DIO 50 days and DPO 38 days, subject to customer, inventory-risk and supplier-policy review.

3 · Choose a responsible first action

Receivables are rising and several invoices are disputed. What should the AR and finance teams do first?

Control notes: DSO, DIO and DPO depend on consistent definitions and period averages. A lower CCC is not automatically better if it comes from stock-outs, unfair payment delays or weakened customer relationships. Reconcile KPI definitions to source data, segment by business unit, and record the owner, due date and evidence for each action.

Accounting Foundations Lab — transactions to statements

Authored practical lesson · beginner level · fictional trading business. Work through the transaction logic, then check your answers. This is a learning example, not a substitute for the applicable accounting framework or tax advice.

1 · The scenario

On 1 April, Nova Supplies starts business. During the month it records these transactions:

  • Owner contributes ₹5,00,000 cash as capital.
  • Business buys office equipment for ₹60,000 cash.
  • Business buys inventory costing ₹90,000 on supplier credit.
  • It sells goods for ₹1,20,000 cash; the goods sold cost ₹70,000.
  • It pays monthly rent of ₹15,000 cash.

2 · The accounting logic

Accounting equation: Assets = Liabilities + Equity. Each transaction must preserve the equation. Under a perpetual-inventory illustration, the sale has two effects: record revenue/cash and separately recognise cost of goods sold while reducing inventory.

Journal entry summary:

  • Dr Cash ₹5,00,000 / Cr Owner's Capital ₹5,00,000.
  • Dr Equipment ₹60,000 / Cr Cash ₹60,000.
  • Dr Inventory ₹90,000 / Cr Accounts Payable ₹90,000.
  • Dr Cash ₹1,20,000 / Cr Sales Revenue ₹1,20,000.
  • Dr Cost of Goods Sold ₹70,000 / Cr Inventory ₹70,000.
  • Dr Rent Expense ₹15,000 / Cr Cash ₹15,000.

Control: Retain invoices, payment proof and authorisation. Confirm the accounting policy, inventory method, cut-off and applicable reporting rules before using a real ledger.

3 · Work the numbers

Closing cash = ₹5,00,000 − ₹60,000 + ₹1,20,000 − ₹15,000 = ₹5,45,000.

Closing inventory = ₹90,000 − ₹70,000 = ₹20,000. Equipment = ₹60,000. Total assets = ₹5,45,000 + ₹20,000 + ₹60,000 = ₹6,25,000.

Accounts payable = ₹90,000. Profit for this simplified example = ₹1,20,000 sales − ₹70,000 cost of goods sold − ₹15,000 rent = ₹35,000. Equity = ₹5,00,000 capital + ₹35,000 profit = ₹5,35,000. Liabilities + equity = ₹90,000 + ₹5,35,000 = ₹6,25,000.

4 · Check your understanding

Workpaper output: Prepare a journal register with date, account, debit, credit, source document, preparer, reviewer and posting reference. Then build a trial balance and reconcile it to supporting schedules. A balanced trial balance alone does not prove that every transaction is correct or complete.

Applied Finance Case Lab — release 1

Practice realistic finance-operations decisions. Each case includes the facts, the calculation/control principle, a knowledge check and feedback. The figures and entities are fictional. These are learning exercises, not real transaction instructions.

CASE 01 · ACCOUNTS PAYABLE

Possible duplicate supplier invoice

Invoice INV-8041 for ₹48,600 is already posted and awaiting payment. A second invoice INV-8041 arrives from the same supplier, with the same date and amount, but a different PDF filename.

Control principle: a different filename is not evidence of a different liability. Compare supplier identity, invoice number, date, amount, purchase order, receipt and posting/payment status before release.

Workpaper output: duplicate-check evidence, matched fields, disposition, reviewer and release/hold decision.

CASE 02 · ACCOUNTS RECEIVABLE

Ageing and collection priority

Three undisputed invoices are overdue: A ₹25,000 for 8 days; B ₹60,000 for 42 days; C ₹35,000 for 75 days. No payment promises or credit holds are recorded. The team must choose which account to review first, using age and value as initial triage signals.

Control principle: ageing and value help prioritise review, but customer risk, dispute status, payment history and policy also matter.

Workpaper output: ageing bucket, amount, customer context, next action, owner and follow-up date.

CASE 03 · BANK RECONCILIATION

Reconcile book and bank balances

General-ledger cash balance is ₹1,89,900. A valid deposit of ₹12,000 is recorded in the books but has not yet appeared on the bank statement. The bank has charged an unrecorded fee of ₹500. No other reconciling items exist.

Method: adjust the book balance for the unrecorded fee; adjust the bank balance for deposits in transit. Both adjusted balances should agree when all items are correctly classified.

Workpaper output: book balance, bank balance, reconciling items, evidence, adjusted balances and reviewer sign-off.

CASE 04 · FP&A / VARIANCE ANALYSIS

Explain an expense variance

Monthly travel expense budget is ₹2,00,000. Actual expense is ₹2,30,000. There are no accrual timing differences in this simplified case.

Formula: variance = actual − budget. For expenses, a positive variance is generally unfavourable; the business explanation still needs evidence.

Workpaper output: amount, favourable/unfavourable label, driver evidence, owner, corrective action and forecast impact.
