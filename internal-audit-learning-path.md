# PHALGUN — Internal Audit Learning Path

**Status:** Curriculum specification; not a claim that every lesson or assessment is already authored.
**Level:** Absolute beginner → practical analyst → professional practice.
**Approach:** Original explanations and exercises, synthetic case data, dated official references, and explicit distinction between internal audit, external audit, compliance, risk management and management ownership.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this path, a learner should be able to explain internal audit's purpose and independence; understand governance, risk and control; map processes; document walkthroughs; assess control design and operating effectiveness; sample and test evidence; write clear findings; agree corrective actions; validate remediation; and communicate results to management and the audit committee.

## Curriculum sequence

1. **Foundations and role** — assurance vs consulting; independence, objectivity, confidentiality, professional scepticism; three lines model as a governance concept; internal audit charter, mandate, authority and access.
2. **Governance, ethics and standards** — board/audit committee oversight, conflicts of interest, quality, due professional care, current applicable Global Internal Audit Standards and local requirements. Always verify the current official edition and effective date.
3. **Risk-based audit planning** — audit universe, risk assessment, materiality/impact, likelihood, risk appetite, annual plan, engagement objectives, scope and resource planning.
4. **Process understanding and walkthroughs** — process narratives, flowcharts, RACI, systems and data flows, key reports, walkthrough evidence, control owners and hand-offs.
5. **Internal control fundamentals** — preventive/detective, manual/automated, entity/process/IT controls, segregation of duties, authorisation, reconciliations, access, change management and monitoring.
6. **Risk and control matrix (RCM)** — objectives, risks, causes/consequences, control descriptions, frequency, owner, evidence, design assessment and test procedures.
7. **Audit fieldwork and evidence** — inquiry, observation, inspection, reperformance, analytics, sampling, reliability, sufficiency, chain of evidence and workpaper documentation.
8. **Testing operating effectiveness** — population completeness, sample rationale, period coverage, exceptions, root cause, impact, control deviation evaluation and review notes.
9. **Finance operations audits** — procure-to-pay/AP, order-to-cash/AR, record-to-report/GL, close, bank and treasury, fixed assets, inventory/costing, payroll, tax-process controls and intercompany.
10. **IT and data controls** — user access, privileged access, joiner/mover/leaver controls, change management, interfaces, backups, logs, cybersecurity basics, spreadsheets, data lineage and automated controls. Technical testing must stay authorised.
11. **Fraud risk and investigations awareness** — red flags, fraud-risk assessment, preserving evidence, escalation and the limits of an internal auditor's investigative authority. No unsupported allegations.
12. **Compliance and regulatory audits** — map obligations to controls, establish jurisdiction and effective dates, verify current official sources, record exceptions and avoid treating a source-page change as proof of a legal change.
13. **Analytics and continuous auditing** — Excel, SQL, Power BI and Python use cases; duplicate payments, split purchases, unusual journals, ageing, access conflicts and outliers; validate data completeness and false positives.
14. **Findings and reporting** — condition, criteria, cause, consequence/risk, recommendation, management response, owner, due date, severity rationale and concise executive reporting.
15. **Issue follow-up and remediation validation** — action tracking, evidence of implementation, retesting, residual risk, overdue escalation and closure approval independent of the action owner.
16. **Communication and stakeholder management** — opening/closing meetings, respectful challenge, difficult conversations, disagreement handling and audit committee reporting.
17. **Quality assurance and audit function improvement** — engagement supervision, workpaper review, quality assessment, performance indicators, stakeholder feedback and improvement plans.
18. **Integrated capstone** — audit a fictional company's month-end close or procure-to-pay process, from planning through walkthrough, RCM, sample testing, findings, management response, remediation retest and final report.

## Required practical exercises

- Build a risk-based audit plan for a fictional small business and a multinational shared-services team.
- Create an RCM and walkthrough pack for supplier invoice processing.
- Test a synthetic payment population for duplicates, split transactions and missing approvals.
- Review month-end journal entries and reconciliations, document exceptions and assess evidence.
- Write one finding using condition, criteria, cause, consequence and corrective action.
- Re-test a remediation claim and decide whether the evidence supports closure.
- Present a concise report to a simulated audit committee.

## Same topic, different business lenses

- **MNC/shared services:** multi-entity controls, ERP access, intercompany, shared-service SLAs and group governance.
- **Indian local corporate:** local processes and applicable statutory requirements, checked against current official sources.
- **Medium-sized business:** limited staff and segregation-of-duties constraints; practical compensating controls.
- **Small industry with foreign clients:** export documentation, FX receipts, inventory and customer credit.
- **Small business owner:** cash, supplier payments, stock, credit sales and owner override risk.
- **Local trader/retailer:** sales and UPI/card settlement, returns, inventory shrinkage and daily cash reconciliation.

## Assessment and quality rules

Each lesson must include a plain-language explanation, worked example, control objective, risk, realistic evidence, guided exercise, answer rationale, knowledge check, and an applied task. Assessments should test judgement—not only definitions—and explain why distractor answers are wrong. Use synthetic data labelled as illustrative. Never imply a real organisation has committed fraud based on a training example.

## Sources and change control

Use current official materials from the Institute of Internal Auditors (IIA), relevant regulators and applicable legal sources. Record title, URL, jurisdiction, version/publication date, checked date, effective date where relevant, and lessons affected. Treat monitoring alerts as review triggers; a qualified review must confirm the change and update lesson content and assessments. Do not reproduce paid or copyrighted exam banks.

## Completion criteria

The pathway is not complete merely because this specification exists. Completion requires authored lessons, practice datasets, answer keys, assessment feedback, working navigation, accessibility checks, current source review, and passing automated/browser acceptance tests.
